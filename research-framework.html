Research-to-Artifact System

A repeatable process for turning ambiguous questions into decision-ready evidence.

Reusable framework, implemented and refined across multiple real research efforts. The evidence process itself is demonstrated here; downstream decision outcomes vary by study and are called out individually below.
View the operating model Representative studies
At a glance

A 10-stage research lifecycle, from problem definition through quality audit to final delivery — skipping a stage requires documenting why, not just moving faster.

A 7-level decision authority hierarchy resolves every judgment call, from explicit instructions down to a bounded, documented assumption.

The convergence loop that produced this page ran 13 real rounds before a confirmation audit found nothing left to fix.

The problem

What ordinary LLM research gets wrong

Jumps from a vague question straight to a polished answer, skipping evidence collection entirely.
Treats its own training data as a source, instead of something that needs a citation.
Confuses a popular opinion with a validated finding, or one good example with proof it generalizes.
Turns a recommendation into a hard requirement without flagging that a judgment call happened.
Delivers a well-written report with no way to trace a claim back to where it actually came from.
Mistakes a completed draft, or a single finished pass, for a completed research process.

Method selection

The method is chosen, not assumed

Artifact-producing problem

Design Science Research Methodology governs by default: problem definition, design, demonstration, evaluation, communication.

Evidence-mapping-only question

A scoping/evidence-synthesis discipline governs instead, without claiming formal systematic-review status unless every requirement is actually met.

Causal question

Neither method applies alone. A causal, experimental, or systematic-review approach is required, and the process says so instead of forcing a fit.

Method turns out to be wrong mid-stream

The method gets replaced. A mismatched method doesn't get carried to the finish line for the sake of momentum.

Evidence & claim discipline

Every source is graded, every claim is classified

A

Primary, authoritative, or peer-reviewed evidence.

B

Strong external/institutional evidence or employer-syndicated primary content.

C

Practitioner guidance or a structured public artifact.

D

Discovery only — can raise a question, can't establish a conclusion.

Source-supported fact Observed finding Estimate Inference Design requirement Design hypothesis Unknown

Four collapses this process is built to prevent:

  • Observation collapsing into causation.
  • Popularity collapsing into validation.
  • Example quality collapsing into effectiveness.
  • A recommendation collapsing into a requirement.
AI & human boundaries

Where the model stops and I start

AI supports

  • Discovery and query generation
  • Extraction and coding suggestions
  • Synthesis across sources
  • Contradiction checks
  • Drafting
  • QA and visual planning

AI may not

  • Treat its own memory as evidence
  • Fabricate support for a claim
  • Silently change methodology mid-stream
  • Hide uncertainty to sound more confident
  • Infer an outcome the data doesn't support
  • Convert my preference into a research fact
  • Make the final approval decision

Human authority retains

  • Defining and approving the actual decision problem
  • Approving consequential assumptions or changes
  • Owning how the final artifact gets used
  • Final decision authority, full stop
Decision authority hierarchy
  1. Explicit instructions
  2. Controlling project authorities
  3. Locked objectives and constraints
  4. Strongest applicable evidence
  5. Reasoned inference
  6. Bounded, documented assumption
  7. Escalation, if a real blocker exists
What actually counts as a blocker

Not routine uncertainty. A real blocker exists only when a decision materially affects validity or usefulness, the available evidence can't resolve it, more than one defensible path remains, and proceeding on an assumption would compromise the research. Everything short of that gets resolved through the hierarchy above, not kicked back as a question.

Disconfirmation & convergence

Execute, find the fault, repair, repeat

Convergence record excerpt

A real excerpt from the process behind this page itself — four rounds out of the thirteen actually run.

Round 1
Fault: "Non-code" described what was absent, not what capability was present. Repair: reframed around Workflow, Decision, and Research/Intelligence system jobs. Delta: high.
Round 4
Fault: a planning case study risked reading as career-advice documentation. Repair: centered the decision architecture, controls, authority, and execution transition instead. Delta: high.
Round 9
Fault: a minimalist homepage risked hiding credibility. Repair: adopted layered progressive disclosure with visible status/evidence cues. Delta: moderate.
Round 13
Confirmation audit. No new substantive structural fault found. Delta: zero — convergence reached.

Outcome & limits

What this proves, and what it doesn't yet

The process above — intake, methodology gate, evidence discipline, convergence, human review — is real and has been refined across multiple real research efforts, this page's own architecture research included. What's still pending is the study-specific evidence: which outcomes were measured versus estimated, what each study's evidence register actually shows, and where the framework itself hit a real limitation rather than a clean success. That detail lands here once the representative studies above are filled in, rather than being asserted ahead of the evidence that would support it.
